“Civic Paper”

The design language for the desktop client. Warm paper chrome over a saturated, living town; a serif for the record, a mono for the money, and one accent reserved for the player's own intent. It exists to be the opposite of the prototype's neon-slate web HUD — not a restyle of it.

1 · Why this language

The problem with the prototype HUD

The PixiJS client's HUD is rgba(12,18,24,.82) panels in cyan-on-slate, system-ui at 13 px. It was built to prove the model, and it reads as a debug overlay that grew: one dark rectangle per subsystem, all equal weight, competing with the map instead of framing it.

Its palette is also the same temperature as the night-time town, so panels dissolve into the world and the player must squint at both.

What the desktop client needs

A game whose depth is the point needs chrome that reads like records: ledgers, certificates, tenancy papers, school letters. Warm paper separates chrome from the town by temperature rather than by shadow, stays legible over grass, brick and dusk, and makes numbers feel accountable — which is exactly the promise of a conserved, ledgered economy.

It also gives every surface a print-like calm that a notification-driven game desperately needs.

2 · Principles (ratifying §7.1)

PrincipleRuling
Legible depthKeep. Every bar or number carries a cause line in the simulation's own words ("last meal 11 h ago", "rent lands before the second wage"). Depth is shown, never summarised away.
Person-firstKeep, and strengthen: the default view is your life. Global metrics never appear in the play surface — they belong to the observer/admin surfaces that already exist.
Desktop-nativeKeep. 1440×900 reference frame, mouse + keyboard, min 1280×720. No responsive reflow; panels may dock, hide or float, never stack like a phone.
Diegetic where possibleKeep. Interiors, markets and jobs are places. A screen that could be a place should be a place.
Quiet by defaultKeep, and make structural: one Town bell digest per session, no pop-ups over the world, no toasts, no modal interrupts. Anything urgent is also a place you can walk to.
New: one accentThe player's own intent is the only thing that may use terracotta. Everything else is information. If the screen looks urgent, the player should be the reason.

Serif for the record, mono for the figure, sans for the interface. Serif is reserved for names, places and chapter titles — the things the town would have written down. Mono is used for every quantity, so digits align in columns without a grid. Tabular figures are mandatory: a balance that reflows when it changes is a balance the player cannot trust.

5 · Space, geometry, elevation

8 px base unit. Spacing is 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 — nothing else. Panel padding is 12 or 14; gutters are 10.

Corners are nearly square (2–4 px). Paper does not have pills. Fully round shapes are reserved for people (portraits, genos dots) and for the intent dial, so roundness means a life.

One hairline, one shadow. Every surface is separated by a 1 px line at #CFC5AF; floating surfaces get a single soft shadow. No inner glows, no double borders, no bevels.

Ledger rows are the universal list: selection is a 3 px accent rule, never a fill.

Buttons state a consequence, not a mood. "Pay at the till · ₡ 40.25" beats "Confirm". Disabled affordances are never silently disabled — they explain themselves ("needs a store", "requires Endurance 5 · you: 3 ✗") and offer the route to becoming available.

7 · Motion

DurationUse
120 msHover, selection, chip state. Ease-out.
180 msPanel enter/exit, tab change, dial open. Ease-out, opacity + 6 px offset.
240 msMode change (world → interior), heir confirmation.
0 msAnything under prefers-reduced-motion. No bounce, no spring, no parallax — ever.

The camera, not the chrome, carries the motion. Panels fade in place; the world moves. A simulation that ticks 25 times a second must never have chrome that also twitches.

8 · Iconography

1.5 px strokes on a 20 px grid, drawn from the world's own objects (a door, a coin, a loaf) rather than generic UI glyphs. Icons are optional: any icon that needs a label to be understood gets the label. Domain icons inherit the domain colour; nothing else is coloured.

9 · Accessibility & input

RequirementRule
ContrastBody text ≥ 7:1 on paper; labels ≥ 4.5:1. Never grey-on-grey for anything the player must read.
Colour independenceEvery domain colour appears with its word ("work", "social"). Needs carry a numeric value and a direction word.
Targets≥ 32 px for mouse, ≥ 44 px on any touch-capable build; the intent dial's items are 132×34 with 8 px gaps.
Focus2 px ink outline, 2 px offset, on every focusable thing. The world canvas is focusable and documents its keys.
Keyboard mapWASD/↑↓←→ move · RMB intend · E interact · Tab household · M map · B bell · Space pause · Esc back. Every dial verb has a chord.
ScalingUI scale 100/125/150 % independent of world zoom; the day ribbon reflows, panels dock.
ReadingAll money and dates are also plain text in the journal export — the game is playable from its record if the chrome is lost.

10 · Anti-patterns (binding)

No web-HUD port. No dark translucent rectangles with a cyan accent, no DOM panel per subsystem, no debug overlay as the default view.
No admin dashboard in play. Gini, vacancy, tick time and population belong to the observer surfaces. The player sees their life.
No number tables as a substitute for a place. If it can be a shelf, a letter or a room, it is not a table.
No notification floods. Nothing appears over the world except the bell's count. No toasts. No "achievement" chrome.
No unexplained state. A bar without a cause line, a disabled button without a reason, or a cost revealed after commitment are all defects.
No decorative colour. A rose-tinted header that means nothing is a lie about the social domain.
