# Population continuity

A closed population dies out. For a simulation meant to run for decades, the world needs a way to replace deaths and respond to labour demand without modelling births and families in V1.

## Immigration as the continuity valve

New working-age NPCs arrive when:

- there are more open jobs than available workers;
- there is vacant housing they can afford;
- the population is below a target;
- the age pyramid is skewed (too few workers per retiree).

Immigrants:

- arrive as adults with a plausible skill profile;
- form or join households;
- enter the labour market as job seekers;
- rent a unit or enter shelter;
- pay taxes and contribute to pensions immediately.

They do not appear from nowhere economically: their arrival adds workers and consumers, and their housing and jobs must exist.

## Why not births

Births require relationships, family formation, childhood dependency and a much longer time horizon. V1 deliberately excludes them. Immigration provides the same function — population replacement and labour supply — with far less complexity.

The four-stage lifecycle still applies to immigrants: they enter at working age and eventually retire and die like everyone else.

## Population targets

The system aims for a stable pyramid:

- 15–20% education;
- 55–60% working age;
- 15–20% retirement;
- 3–7% elderly.

If the working-age share falls too low, immigration increases. If housing vacancy is too low, immigration slows until supply catches up. If unemployment is high, immigration stops.

## Failure modes

- **Open borders without housing** → homeless arrivals and shelter overflow.
- **Immigration without jobs** → unemployment and fiscal strain.
- **No immigration** → demographic collapse after the first generation.
- **Immigration as a free money source** → new consumers with no capital distort the economy; arrivals start with modest cash, not wealth.

## Admin controls

- set the target population and age mix;
- trigger an arrival wave;
- pause or resume immigration;
- inspect arrivals, their skills, jobs and housing outcomes;
- see the demographic pyramid over time.

## Out of scope

Births, family reunification, refugees, emigration, visa policy and cultural variation. V1 needs a stable population, not a migration model.
