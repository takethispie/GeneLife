# Working life

Working life is the economic engine of the population. It runs from 18 to 65 and determines income, savings, housing, tax and eventual pension.

## The working day

A worker has a schedule, not a permanent state:

- shifts occupy part of the day;
- needs still decay and must be met around work;
- critical needs can interrupt a shift;
- attendance and performance accumulate over time.

This makes work a constraint on behaviour rather than a label.

## Finding work

A worker enters the labour market with a skill profile and a desired salary. The hiring system matches them to open seats by skill fit and salary fit. Unemployment is a real state:

- income stops, savings drain, housing is at risk;
- a benefit provides a floor, funded by the government;
- after a configured period, the worker may accept a lower salary or move.

## Career progression

Work is not static. Over years:

- attendance and performance produce a performance score;
- strong performers are promoted to higher seniority and salary;
- weak performers receive warnings and can be separated;
- skills improve slowly on the job;
- salary bands rise with seniority and market conditions.

Promotions create vacancies below them, which keeps the labour market moving.

## Leaving work

A worker leaves employment by:

- resignation (better job, burnout, long commute in a future version);
- firing (performance or company distress);
- retirement at 65;
- death.

Every separation frees a seat, ends wage income and starts a transition — to job seeking, pension or estate.

## What realism requires

- A worker cannot instantly find a perfect job.
- A bad employer is visible through unpaid wages and arrears.
- A long unemployment spell damages savings and housing stability.
- A career produces a rising income curve, not a flat wage forever.
- Retirement income is meaningfully lower than working income.

## Admin controls

- force hire or fire;
- freeze hiring at a company;
- adjust salary bands and promotion thresholds;
- trigger a resignation or retirement for scenario testing;
- inspect attendance, performance, tenure and wage history per worker.
