# Why social play

A shared world is more interesting when the people in it can acknowledge each other. V3 proved that multiple players can share one server-authoritative town. V5 makes that presence meaningful without turning the game into a social network.

## What social play adds

- **Recognition.** A player can see another player, learn their name and remember them.
- **Cooperation.** Players can meet at a venue, talk and form a friendship.
- **Shared stories.** A player partnership or a friendship creates a relationship that the simulation can remember.
- **A reason to return.** Other people make the town less predictable than NPCs alone.
- **A test of the world.** Social behavior exposes whether housing, venues, appearance and identity are readable in play.

## What social play is not

- It is not a global chat room.
- It is not a dating app or a matchmaking service.
- It is not a social feed with likes, followers or public profiles.
- It is not voice or video.
- It is not a reason to expose private family, wallet or utility state.

## Design consequence

Start with the smallest social surface that makes the world feel inhabited: nearby presence, local chat, emotes, friends, blocks and a consensual partnership. Add venues and activities only where the existing map already gives players a reason to be together.

## What the player should notice

- Other players are visible and recognizable.
- They can talk locally and add a friend.
- They can ignore or block someone without leaving the world.
- A player partnership is a choice, not an automatic side effect of proximity.
