# Why appearance matters

V4 gave every sim an inherited trait set, but the browser still draws a coloured circle. The player cannot see family resemblance, cannot recognize their own child and cannot tell one NPC from another except by position and colour.

Appearance turns genetics into something the player can read at a glance.

## What appearance adds

- **Identity.** A sim becomes a person with a face, not a token.
- **Family resemblance.** A child looks like their parents because they inherit the same traits, so the family graph becomes visible.
- **Aging.** A sim changes over a lifetime without becoming a different person.
- **Recognition.** The player can find their partner, child or coworker in a crowd.
- **A reason to care about traits.** V4's genetics were invisible outside the admin page; appearance gives them meaning in play.

## What appearance is not

- It is not a beauty score or a public ranking.
- It is not a character creator in V5a.
- It is not a wardrobe or cosmetic marketplace.
- It is not a separate genetic system; it is derived from the existing traits.
- It is not a per-sim texture or a portrait file stored for every sim.

## Design consequence

The smallest useful version is a compact descriptor and a layered sprite. A few trait-driven parts and palettes produce enough variety to recognize families without an asset explosion or a client-side genetics engine.

## What the player should notice

- Their sim has a consistent look that survives save/restore and reconnect.
- Their child resembles them and their partner in a few visible ways.
- Older sims look older without losing their identity.
- Different families have recognizable visual tendencies over generations.
