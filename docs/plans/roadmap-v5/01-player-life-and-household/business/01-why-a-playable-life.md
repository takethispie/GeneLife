# Why a playable life

V1–V4 built a town that can run without the player. Sims work, rent, age, partner, have children, inherit and die. The player can walk through that town and use a few buildings, but cannot take part in the systems that make it interesting.

V5 changes the player from an observer into a resident.

## What a playable life adds

- **A reason to care.** A job, a home, a partner and a child make the simulation personal. A wage cut, an eviction or a birth matters because it happens to the player's household.
- **A use for the existing systems.** Payroll, rent, arrears, relationships, pregnancy, inheritance and estates already exist. The player should experience them, not watch them from outside.
- **Emergent stories.** A player who loses a job, moves to a cheaper unit and starts a family is producing a story from rules the NPCs already follow.
- **A foundation for appearance and social play.** A player identity with a job, home and family is what makes a portrait, a friendship or a shared venue meaningful.

## What a playable life is not

- It is not a second game mode with different rules.
- It is not client-authoritative movement, money or family state.
- It is not a power fantasy where the player ignores rent, needs or consent.
- It is not a full life-sim UI with every V1–V4 system exposed on day one.

## Design consequence

The smallest useful version exposes one complete loop: get a job, earn money, rent a home, buy food, form a relationship, have a child and continue after death. Each step uses the existing domain service and ledger. Extra depth can be added later without changing the authority model.

## What the player should notice

- Their choices have consequences in the same economy as the NPCs.
- A partner and a child are relationships, not menu flags.
- A home is a cost and a constraint, not a free spawn point.
- Death is part of the story, and the family can continue.
