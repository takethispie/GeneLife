# Why births

Today the population replaces itself through immigration. Every new adult arrives from outside, and the age pyramid is a policy output. Births change that: families grow from within, children inherit from parents, and the population can continue without a constant inflow.

## What births add

- **Internal continuity.** A population can replace itself without relying only on immigration.
- **Family consequence.** Partnership, housing, income and health now have a next generation to affect.
- **A real age pyramid.** Births and deaths produce a shape that immigration alone cannot.
- **Long-run testability.** A 30-year run can assert birth rate, replacement rate and dependency ratio.
- **Meaningful inheritance.** A family can pass on traits, housing and assets to children.

## What births are not

- They are not a player pregnancy minigame.
- They are not a fertility score or a population target that overrides individual state.
- They are not a source of free money or free housing.
- They are not an excuse to skip parent edges or household membership.

## The minimum believable birth

A newborn should be a normal sim:

- age 0, education stage, dependent tenure;
- inherited traits and an aptitude;
- a guardian household and a residence;
- parent edges to both parents;
- zero wallet and normal needs;
- enrolled at school age, not at birth;
- subject to the same health, housing and money rules as everyone else.

If any of those is missing, later systems will special-case children forever.

## What the operator should see

- active pregnancies, due dates and risk;
- births and losses per year;
- the parents and guardian of each child;
- household size and overcrowding;
- school enrollment and the eventual transition to work;
- child costs and who pays them.
